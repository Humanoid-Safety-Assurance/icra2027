01 / THE WORKSHOP

Safety after deployment

Policy updates, online adaptation, sensor recalibration, and actuator wear can change a humanoid robot’s behavior after deployment. New payloads, tools, workflows, and sites can also affect the assumptions supporting its safety claims. Whole-body motion, intermittent contacts, and close human interaction make these changes relevant across perception, learning, and control.

This proposed workshop asks which earlier safety evidence remains applicable after a change, which assumptions need to be checked again, and what additional analysis or testing is needed. Researchers in robot learning, whole-body control, verification, HRI, and deployment will examine how this evidence can inform decisions about continued operation.

02 / DISCUSSION

Three questions to work through

  1. Which safety assumptions, robot capabilities, and safety claims are affected by a change?
  2. Which earlier evidence can be reused, and when are new analyses, runtime checks, policy evaluations, or physical tests needed?
  3. What evidence supports continued or restricted operation, rollback, human handover, or suspension pending further checks?

A proposed shared case

A warehouse humanoid after an update

A humanoid transfers sealed totes to a table. Workers wait for tote release and arm withdrawal before approaching. A manipulation-policy update, relocation beside an occluded pedestrian aisle, and earlier human access change this operating setup. Which assumptions and safety evidence need to be checked again?

  1. Trace the change. Identify affected claims and assumptions.
  2. Examine the evidence. Compare what can be reused with what needs new analysis or testing.
  3. Discuss the decision. State the conditions for continued operation, restrictions, rollback, handover, or suspension.

What the discussion would produce

Each group would record the change, affected claims and assumptions, evidence to reuse or regenerate, and an operational decision with a fallback and recheck trigger. Groups would retain evidence gaps and competing arguments. The closing panel would identify observations or tests that could resolve disagreements. We plan to share reviewed records, case materials, and open research questions after the workshop.

05 / CONTRIBUTIONS

Research, deployment cases, and open questions

We plan to welcome methods, deployment case studies, position papers, negative results, and tools from academic, industrial, and independent researchers.

Contributions should explain a concrete change, the safety claims or assumptions it affects, the evidence needed to reassess them, and how that evidence informs an operational decision.

Planned submission routes

Papers. We plan to invite 2–4-page non-archival papers, plus references. Two non-conflicted reviewers would assess scientific merit and the connection between changes, safety claims, evidence, and operational decisions.

Fresh ideas. Researchers could submit a brief abstract for lightning-talk consideration without an accepted paper. Selected paper authors and fresh-idea researchers would share up to ten six-minute slots. Paper acceptance would not guarantee a lightning slot.

The call for contributions, submission instructions, and deadlines will be announced after workshop acceptance. Submissions are not yet open.

Topics of interest

  • Policy updates, adaptation, and safe robot learning
  • Whole-body control, sensing, and changing operating conditions
  • Formal verification and runtime assurance
  • Human interaction, oversight, and handover
  • Safety testing, benchmarking, and evidence reuse

Early-career researchers and first-time contributors are encouraged to participate.
